Maldives Trademark Law Takes Effect: 12-Month Transitional Period, Newspaper Notices No Longer Confer Rights

November 12, 2026 · Foreign trademarks · Maldives · Najie IP

Yesterday, November 11, 2026, the Maldives' first Trademark Law officially came into force. The law is numbered Law No. 19 (2025), approved and published on November 11 last year, and took a full year to be implemented.

For more than a decade, Chinese companies protecting their brands in this Indian Ocean island nation relied on a method that sounds a bit unreliable today: paying to publish a cautionary notice in a local newspaper. In Dhivehi and English, translation plus publication took 15 to 20 working days, and the cost varied with newspaper space, with no uniform price. Once published, the brand was considered protected.

Starting yesterday, that practice no longer creates rights.

The new law moves trademark protection from newspapers into the official register. A few hard rules to remember first: the first-to-file principle, whoever files first gets the right; registration is valid for 10 years and can be renewed indefinitely; if a registered trademark is not genuinely used for five consecutive years, others may apply for its revocation. For counterfeiting, the Maldives imposes fines of MVR 100,000 to 2,000,000, and border enforcement measures have been added.

To be honest, when I first saw this news, my first reaction was "good, finally there is law to rely on." But thinking further, problems emerge: what should clients who still hold old newspaper notices do now?

The answer is 364 days. The deadline is November 11, 2027, by which you must "migrate" old rights into the new register. If you fail to migrate, the rights lapse.

Three things you can do right now

First, take stock. Do it within this month; the cost is almost zero. Go through distribution contracts, invoices and bills of lading, packaging labels, hotel cooperation documents, and identify trademarks actually used in the Maldives. The most critical step is to request scanned copies of the original notices from the agents of that time. This old newspaper is the only written evidence of your prior use under the new law, and it is usually not in your own hands.

Second, re-register. Do not delay beyond November 11, 2027; the earlier the better. Avoid two pitfalls: first, file in your own name, not under the local distributor's name—brands in tourism, hotel, and diving services are most vulnerable here; second, re-check the classes. Old notices had no class restrictions; one notice could cover all classes. The new law examines by class, and the comfortable days of broad coverage are over.

Third, keep records. After registration, keep evidence of use from day one: sales records, advertising placements, customs clearance documents, platform page screenshots, archived by year. Because if you do not use the mark for five consecutive years, others can apply for revocation.

A counterintuitive reminder

The implementing regulations—fee schedules and specific procedures—are expected to be issued only before May 2027. Some might think: then wait until the regulations come out before acting.

Do not wait. Under the first-to-file principle, the filing date is ranked by submission order. If you delay until the regulations are out, you are effectively putting your filing date behind others. The fact that fees are temporarily unknown is not a reason to delay; it is precisely the reason to act now.

One more cold shower: do not read this single trademark law as the entire intellectual property system. The Maldives has no statute law for patents, industrial designs, or geographical indications to date. If a client asks to "also register the design while at it," the answer is: you cannot apply trademark logic.

Three dates, please remember them accurately, and make sure your agent remembers them too: approved on November 11, 2025; effective on November 11, 2026; transitional period ends on November 11, 2027. Some people online have already conflated the effective date and the deadline into one date; be vigilant when you see such statements.

Newspaper notices no longer equal rights—this sentence takes effect from yesterday. How many old notices you hold equals how many 364-day countdowns you have.
