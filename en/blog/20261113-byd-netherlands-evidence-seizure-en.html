Dutch Raid Set Aside After Three Months: Three Steps Every Going-Global Company Should Know

November 13, 2026 · Cross-border IP · Evidence seizure · Mili Law Firm

In August this year, two of BYD's offices in Europe were raided under a court warrant; entire sets of digital records were copied and sealed. Three months later, on November 11, the Hague court itself set aside that search warrant.

During those three months, the materials remained locked with a third party appointed by the court, and neither side could access them. This is precisely the point in the whole affair that outbound companies should pause and think about.

What Happened: Approved First, Set Aside Later

First, let us be clear about what happened. Jaguar Land Rover (JLR) sued BYD in The Hague in June this year, alleging that the Fang Cheng Bao Ti7 (known overseas as Ti7) infringes the design of the Land Rover Defender, and suspecting reverse engineering. In August, the court first approved the search on an ex parte basis, without notifying BYD, and sent a bailiff straight through the door. In October, the two sides clashed for the first time over "which records may be used." On November 11, the Hague court ruled to set aside the seizure order, giving two reasons: the broad-scale surprise evidence gathering lacked sufficient necessity; and the Ti7 has not yet been launched in the EU, so the connection is insufficient.

One point must be stated precisely: it is not that "the court never approved the search," but rather approved first, set aside later. Several Chinese reports headlined it as "rejecting the evidence preservation application," which easily reads as though it was never approved in the first place. The real timing gap is that the raid has already happened, and your battleground is the subsequent objection proceedings. This is also the only useful information for outbound companies.

Dutch Article 1019 Rv: Three Features

This Dutch move rests on Article 1019 and following of the Dutch Code of Civil Procedure, the special chapter on IP enforcement. Note three features: with prima facie evidence, the court may order interim measures to preserve evidence; seized evidence must remain confidential, the requesting party has no right to use it, and must bring a separate proceeding to do so; Article 1019h provides that the losing party in an IP case must compensate the other side's actual reasonable costs, far higher than the fixed small sums awarded at liquidated rates in ordinary cases. According to media reports, BYD was awarded approximately EUR 25,000 in litigation costs this time, and the legal basis is precisely this article.

Corresponding Provisions in Chinese Law: Four Factors and Lifting by Objection

Chinese law actually has one-to-one corresponding provisions for this framework. Article 84 of the Code of Civil Procedure governs evidence preservation; Article 11 of the Supreme People's Court's Provisions on Evidence in Intellectual Property Cases sets out the four-factor review, Article 12 requires "limited to effectively fixing the evidence" and graduated measures, and Article 17 allows the respondent to raise an objection, upon which the court may modify, terminate, or lift the preservation.

The two reasons given by the Hague court, necessity and connection, and the "impact on the holder" among the four factors in Article 11, are almost the same language. Chinese companies may not be familiar with the Dutch Rv, but they are familiar with the framework of "necessity + least infringement + lifting upon objection." That is the bridge.

Three Practical Steps: On Site, Objection, Costs

Back to action, there are just three steps.

Step one, on site. Have counsel attend to verify the scope of enforcement, compare the seizure list item by item, and document on the spot any overbroad scope.

Step two, objection. Do not expect that arguing forcefully on site will stop it. In Dutch practice, "fishing" defenses are rarely accepted at the seizure stage; the real contest is at the disclosure stage. Assert two points in writing: insufficient necessity and insufficient connection, while also seeking use restrictions and confidentiality.

Step three, costs. Claim actual costs (1019h); where the preservation is set aside, the applicant may be liable for damages.

This toolkit is not only available to foreign parties. Chinese companies can likewise apply for evidence preservation against a foreign party's entity in China, under Article 84 of the Code of Civil Procedure plus the IP evidence provisions. Offense and defense are structurally symmetrical.

A Bucket of Cold Water: This Is Only a Procedural Ruling

Finally, a bucket of cold water: November 11 is only a procedural ruling, not a substantive judgment. Following the pace of precedent, the substantive outcome is measured in years. Do not read this procedural round as "who won."

In handling cross-border disputes, Mili repeatedly encounters one point: the success of overseas evidence preservation turns on the written arguments during the objection period, not on the moment on site. Najie offers companies preparing to go global an earlier piece of advice: your place of registration is where you will defend lawsuits in the future, so think it through before you make your move.
