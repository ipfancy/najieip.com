Six Compliance Lessons from a Successful Antitrust Defense: The SPC's Non-Binding Recommendations in a Gas Utility Case

October 6, 2026 · Antitrust & Public Utilities · Mili Law Firm / Aipunajie Patent Firm

A gas company was sued. All three allegations of abuse of dominant market position failed. The second-instance court dismissed the appeal and upheld the original judgment. Complete victory.

But at the end of that judgment, the Supreme People's Court devoted an entire paragraph to listing its "room for further improvement"—six items in total.

These six items are not part of the operative part of the judgment and have no enforceability. They will become the most readily available citation material for the plaintiff in the next case.

(2025) SPC Zhi Min Zhong No. 950. This article gives you three things: the original text of the six-item list, the implementation actions and evidence carriers for each item, and a timing trap that can turn "done" into "not done."

I. The Six-Item List

In its reasoning, the Supreme People's Court stated that the gas company, as the sole operating enterprise responsible for providing natural gas supply in Tengchong, Yunnan, "under the goal of ensuring a local balance of total supply and demand, still has room for further improvement in how to more effectively achieve advance forecasting and precise planning," and should subsequently take further necessary measures to strengthen the standardization and transparency of its business management.

Here are the six specific items, broken down by "requirement → action → evidence":

# SPC requirement Action Evidence carrier
1 Intensify outreach to downstream users Hold a mechanism briefing once a year covering the declaration cycle, adjustment conditions, deviation settlement formula and unit price Sign-in sheets + materials + photos/video
2 Stabilize the predictability of the annual declaration start date Fix the start date and give written notice at least 15 days in advance Notice letter + group announcement screenshot + delivery record
3 Publicly inform users that adjustments can still be requested after submission Write "adjustments possible" into the contract or a written notice Contract clause + signed acknowledgment of notice
4 Publicly inform users of the conditions for monthly adjustments Written rules specifying acceptance conditions and exceptions User manual + website/group announcement
5 Publicly disclose the method and process for inter-user transfers Application—review—three-party on-site confirmation, with the process made public Process document + three-party confirmation record
6 Jointly analyze causes with persistently deviating users and propose suggestions Communicate whenever deviation persists for 3 consecutive months Communication records + written suggestions

II. The Hardest Part Is Not Doing It, but Retaining Evidence

None of the six items is difficult in itself. Item 3 is even just a matter of one sentence: tell users "you can still adjust after submitting."

The hard part is evidence retention. How did the plaintiff in this case attack? It argued that the gas company's inter-user transfer mechanism "occurred after the lawsuit was filed and has no retroactive effect," that it was "reactive remediation under litigation pressure," and it submitted WeChat group records claiming that as of 30 December 2024, none of the 12 industrial enterprises in the park knew of the mechanism.

In other words: the existence of a mechanism does not equal its usability. The standard of proof for usability is "all users were explicitly informed, and there is a trace."

The same act, done early, is called proactive compliance building; done late, it is evidence of opportunism. Timing determines nature.

III. A Concrete Scenario

Why does Item 2 emphasize "predictability of the start date"? This scenario makes it clear.

At 8:40 am on 22 October 2024, gas company staff posted a notice in the "Industrial Month-End Settlement Group": due to an urgent notice from upstream, they needed to once again collect and report next year's gas consumption plans, and asked users to send electronic versions before 9:30 am that day, adding "time is tight, thanks for your effort."

Responses in the group included: "We can't report accurately and have no way to adjust, no way to report, can't report, no solution to real difficulties."

Notice given the same day, deadline in 50 minutes. This scene was written into the judgment—precisely what Item 2 is meant to change.

IV. Self-Check Checklist

Check against your own situation. Whatever you cannot check is your weakest point when sued.

  • Annual briefing has been held, with sign-in or photo/video records
  • Annual declaration has a fixed start date, with written notice at least 15 days in advance
  • The contract or written notice states that "adjustments can still be requested after submission"
  • Monthly adjustment conditions are set out in written rules and accessible to users
  • The inter-user transfer process is documented in writing and known to all users
  • There are records of proactive communication with persistently deviating users

V. Beyond the List: Three Things That Actually Decided the Case

Copying the list is easy, but the list is not why the defendant won. The defendant won on three pieces of evidence retention:

First, the sealed "Energy Demand Plan Confirmation Form," with new seals confirming each of the three volume adjustments. Second, a three-and-a-half-year ledger of upstream-downstream deviation reconciliations, which turned "RMB 390,000 in profit" into "RMB 13,000 in difference." Third, the evidence package for suspension of supply: demand notices, verification of performance of effective legal documents, credit inquiry reports (stating the time of inquiry), and written approvals.

The list addresses "procedurally, you did everything." These three address "substantively, you stand on solid ground."
