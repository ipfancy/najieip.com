2. Why three entities: separate licences, not added complexity

Trademark agency work requires trademark agency filing; patent agency work requires registration as a patent agency (IPNAJIE, agency code 11419); litigation and general counsel require a law firm practice licence. The three licences are independent, and none of them may take on the work reserved to another.

What this means for a client: every document you receive is issued by an entity holding the corresponding licence. From patent filing to infringement litigation, the responsible entity stays clear and the chain of qualification is never broken. The division of labour is not meant to make you deal with three providers — it is what keeps a licensed professional behind every stage of your rights.

6. Process and delivery

From first contact to delivery, the process follows six fixed steps:

  1. Instruction: clarify the type of work, the goods or technology involved, and the target markets;
  2. Search and assessment: trademark similarity search or patent novelty search, with a written assessment;
  3. Fee proposal: professional fees and official fees listed separately, transparently, with no hidden charges;
  4. Preparation and filing: official electronic filing, with a complete record of every step;
  5. Case management: proactive notice at every office action, opposition or refusal, with a proposed response;
  6. Delivery and follow-up: certificate delivery, and reminders for annuities and renewals.

Timing: first drafts of new patent applications are returned in 5–7 working days on the standard track, 3–4 working days on the expedited track and 1–2 working days on the urgent track; for infringement threats, a preliminary comparison opinion is issued within 48 hours. These are normal working timings and exclude the time needed for client confirmation and official examination.
